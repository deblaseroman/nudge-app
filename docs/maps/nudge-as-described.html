1.0The sentence

Nudge is a personal assistant for a student with ADHD. It structures what they dumped, plans their day, builds the steps for what matters, reminds them at the right moment, and prepares them for what is ahead.

For

Anyone, designed for students with ADHD.

Against

Executive dysfunction, task paralysis, forgetfulness, and planning. The app does the parts those make hard: it decides what to do next, when to be reminded, and what preparing for a big thing actually consists of.

How

Three verbs. Nudge: the right notification for the user's tasks and events. Plan the day: put today's work on the timeline in a sensible order. Plan the thing: for an important due-dated task or event, propose the subtasks that prepare for or complete it, and add them only when the user says yes.

Ceiling

Under $10 of API per user per month. Simplicity wins ties. No conversation that is not about tasks, events, settings, or plans.

2.0A task has two clocks, and they mean different things

This is where you said the mix-up lived, and I agree it is the center of everything else. A due date is an anchor: the moment the task is owed, set by the user or by the calendar, never moved by the app. A scheduled time is an intention: when the user, or Plan my day, decided to work on it. Passing a scheduled time is not being late. Passing the due date is.

3.0The shapes a task can take, and where each one lives

Crossing the two clocks gives four shapes. Each has a home, a way it reaches the timeline, and a rule for what happens when it is not done. Events are the anchored shape with no work attached: they are attended, not completed, and the app never moves them.

4.0The core loop

Two doors in, one path after. A brain dump and a calendar import land in the same place and are treated the same way from there: listed, organized, ranked in the Tasks tab, placed by Plan my day, worked through Start Session. Order and color are one truth shared by the list, the widget, and the timeline.

5.0The plan: prepare the user, but ask first

A plan is the app building the subtasks that prepare for or complete something important and necessary. It exists only for a task or event with a due date. Opus explains it in the message box; a Yes adds the subtasks to the list and the timeline; a No changes nothing. This is the "prepare the user for what is ahead" verb, and it is the same shape whether the thing is an exam, a trip, or an application.

7.0Rulings, and what is still open

Roman answered the same day. Every entry is now a rule.

  • A floater is a filler.
    Everyday tasks with little penalty if they slip: laundry, dishes, cleaning, picking up a package. Usually dumped as "I want to do that tomorrow". Less important than a project or an application, still worth listing. Placed when the user has spare time or has already been productive.
  • Skipped means: on Today at the user's request, not completed.
    After two days a single task with no due date, and not a subtask of an anchored thing, goes to a new Skipped section, separate from Overdue. A subtask of an anchored thing can be skipped but is neither rescheduled nor sent to either section.
  • Exams: the user's own study task wins; otherwise the app asks.
    If the user made a study task for an exam, the app follows what they said. Otherwise the AI in the chat asks whether to build a study plan; a No means the app adds only what the user asked for.
  • The Opus memo stays, and grows the plan.
    Fixing the memo's copy is a later process. Now: it gains the ability to propose subtasks for anchored tasks and events, and the AI must read each one and judge whether it is worth a plan at all.
  • The arbiter and its ten builders stay as they are.
  • Importance: the existing ranking stays for anything without a due date.
    Only things with a Due Date use the ladder: medium from the day they are created, high on the day they are due. Among due-dated items the soonest anchor comes first, as the shared order already does. Nothing else about the ranking changes.
