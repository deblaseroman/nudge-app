The Ten Builders

Every notification Nudge can send comes from exactly one of ten builders. Each proposes candidates for one situation, at one anchor in the day — and each is measured separately, because its outcome kind is its analytics identity.

1.0Where the day's nudges anchor

Nothing fires at a fixed clock time. Six builders anchor at an offset from the user's wake time, so the whole rhythm shifts with their schedule; the other four anchor to a thing — an event, a deadline, a timeline slot — and fire relative to it. If an anchor has already passed when the arbiter runs, it rolls to tomorrow.

2.0The catalog

EXEMPT builders state facts — they skip the shared gates and the daily budget, self-checking only what applies to them, and are seeded into the winner set first. COUNTS builders make suggestions — they pass every gate and compete for 10 budget slots at 90-minute spacing.

01

Event blocks

eventBlock
EXEMPT — a fact
Fires
60 min before the first event of a cluster (back-to-back events share one heads-up)
Targets
Today's calendar events
Notable
Deliberately actionless — an FYI before class wants no decision from you. The one notification kind Roman kept.

"Class in 1 hour."

02

Morning prompt

morningPrompt
EXEMPT — the day's anchor
Fires
Wake + 30 min
Targets
Names the day's biggest open task, ranked by stakes
Notable
Skips packed days; a task steps aside after 2 straight mornings; repeats rather than naming something a whole tier less important. Tap lands in Home chat.

"Biggest thing today: finish the essay."

03

Idle check-in

idle
COUNTS
Fires
Wake + 3 h — if no session started and nothing completed
Targets
The plan's next item, else the top-scored task
Notable
The paralysis-breaker. Its "Not yet" reply survives cold launches via a durable app-group handoff.

"Want to start with a 10-minute session?"

04

Prep — "start early"

prep
COUNTS
Fires
Wake + 2 h on a planner-picked day before a deadline
Targets
Deadline work worth starting ahead of time
Notable
The fire day comes from StartByPlanner; the walk stops at the deadline — never nudges "get ahead" on something already late.

"Good day to start on the lab report."

05

Due soon — "this is landing"

dueSoon
EXEMPT — a fact
Fires
2 h before a real deadline, once per task ever
Targets
Only genuine deadlines — never intentions, never generated study days
Notable
Deadlines in the same hour batch into one notification. Since the deadline/intent split, this can no longer fire on fabricated pressure.

"Essay due at 11:59 tonight."

06

Floater check-in

floater
COUNTS
Fires
Wake + 6 h
Targets
Open tasks with no deadline and no upcoming intent day — including intentions whose day slipped
Notable
Starved for months because capture dated everything; the intent split created its population. Wake+6h is a known-bad anchor, parked until outcome data exists.

"'Organize desk' is still open — got a slot for it?"

07

Come-back

comeBack
COUNTS
Fires
3 quiet days after last engagement, at wake + 2 h
Targets
The user, not a task — names the nearest upcoming thing
Notable
Scheduled on every run; any engagement pushes it forward, so it only ever fires for someone genuinely away. Forward-looking copy only — never guilt.

"'Chem quiz' is coming up Friday. Your list is ready when you are."

08

Goal lapse

goalLapse
COUNTS
Fires
30 days after a goal's last activity, wake + 8 h, at most monthly per goal
Targets
One neglected personal goal per run
Notable
Deliberate bait — near-contentless; the real message waits in the Tasks message box the tap opens, with a one-tap "20 minutes on it" offer.

"Got a minute?"

09

Placement heads-up

placementLead
COUNTS
Fires
5 min before a timeline slot; close slots share one
Targets
Tasks you placed (or scheduled by saying "at 9am")
Notable
Urgency floored high — your own plan slipping is the urgency signal.

"Python session in 5 minutes."

10

Placement follow-up

placementMissed
COUNTS
Fires
30 min after a slot passes unstarted, then every 2 h, max 4 tries, ends at midnight
Targets
The slot that slipped
Notable
Pre-scheduled as a series so it works even if the app never runs. Completing or unscheduling the task ends it declaratively.

"The 12:00 Python block slipped — still doable today?"

Why exactly one outcome kind per builder: the kind is the measurement identity. Two features once shared a kind, and their rows were indistinguishable — neither could be evaluated. Retired kinds you may see in old data: getAhead (split into prep + dueSoon) and break-it-down (removed entirely; do not resurrect).

3.0Why two lanes exist at all

The split is a product stance, not an implementation detail: facts about the world (a class is starting, a deadline is landing) must always arrive — silencing them requires an explicit per-kind toggle. Suggestions (start something, check in, come back) compete under a budget precisely so the app never becomes the thing an overwhelmed user learns to ignore. Every builder in the second lane can stand down entirely and say nothing — a quiet day is a valid output.
