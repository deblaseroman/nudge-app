"i need to pick up my friend from the airport 5 days from now at 3pm"

Typed on a Sunday. Every box this sentence passes through below is a system documented in the companion maps — this page shows them composing: the router's economics, the model's judgment, the schema's honesty, the day-glue, the deterministic scheduler, and the outcome record that closes the loop.

1.0The first five seconds — from language to a fact

2.0Five quiet days — glued to Saturday, visible everywhere, touched by nothing

3.0Saturday — the machine does its one job, then remembers

4.0The whole journey, numbered

  1. Typed. One sentence in Home chat, Sunday afternoon.
  2. Routed. The deterministic gate sees digits — this is work, not chitchat. Costs nothing to decide.
  3. Understood. Opus 5 (effort low, ~6¢, ~4s): fixed time + another person = an event; "5 days from now" copied from the generated date table, never computed.
  4. Written, distrustfully. Schema-validated, every potential drop logged, counts reconciled. The model is treated as a talented, fallible colleague.
  5. Confirmed. A one-line reply naming the day and time — the user can catch a misread immediately.
  6. Glued. For five days: visible on the calendar, Events tab, and week lens; untouchable by every "what should I do now" path.
  7. Drawn. Saturday: a stone block at 3 PM on the timeline strip — which stretches to show it even outside waking hours.
  8. Scheduled. The arbiter's event-block builder emits the T−60m heads-up in the exempt lane: facts skip budgets and gates.
  9. Fired. 2:00 PM, exact clock time in the body, no AI in the delivery path.
  10. Remembered, correctly. The outcome row records the delivery; the classifier knows that for this kind, silence ≠ ignored. The feedback loop stays honest.
