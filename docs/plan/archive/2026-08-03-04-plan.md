At 10:30pm a commitment was captured and silently generated a first session

tomorrow**, not started today anyway.

Say what rule you used.

## 2 — The interactive message box (shell)

The message box gains an expanded state, roughly like iOS's swipe-down
reply on a notification: tapping the character opens a larger surface with
the conversation and an input, tapping outside collapses it back.

**Collapsed** — as today, plus the character in the slot.

**Expanded** — conversation history, a text field, and tappable options
when the app has asked something. Options and free text both available.

**Stub everything behind it.** Hard-coded messages are fine, a fake
question with two options is fine, no persistence needed. I want to judge
the interaction, not the plumbing.

**Do not touch Home chat.** It stays exactly as it is. This is a parallel
surface so I can compare them.

**Do not wire the real capture pipeline into it.** That's a separate
cycle and a much bigger one.

The character stays the grey placeholder — a messenger pigeon goes there
later.

## Constraints

- `NudgeTheme` only.
- Nothing in `TasksMessageBox`'s existing composer states should break —
  the collapsed box must still show what it shows today.
- No new `@Model`, no persistence, no API calls.
- **Timebox this.** If the expand/collapse animation or the keyboard
  handling turns into a fight, ship it rough and say so. A rough version I
  can look at beats a polished one that took three times as long.

## Evidence it worked

- Both schemes build.
- Tapping the character expands the box; tapping outside collapses it.
- The expanded state shows stub messages, a text field, and two tappable
  options.
- The collapsed state is unchanged from today.

## Out of scope

- Wiring real capture, real questions, or real answers.
- Removing or changing Home chat.
- Persistence, history, the mascot asset.
- Merging or pushing.
