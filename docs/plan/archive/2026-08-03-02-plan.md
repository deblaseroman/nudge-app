## 1 — Today's session is born overdue

Every commitment's **first day** reads "20 hours ago." The task the user is
meant to work on *today* is already past due the moment it's created.

Find where the generator sets `dueDate` for each day's task and report what
it's actually setting before changing it — a bare date normalising to
midnight is the likely cause, but confirm rather than assume.

The rule: **a session's due time is the end of its own day.** Today's
session is due tonight, not this morning.

Also confirm the day count. The screenshot shows Python sessions on Aug 3,
4, 5, 6 and at least one more, where 8 hours across the days to Friday
should produce four. Report what the generator computed and why — an
off-by-one in the day range would explain both the count and possibly the
due-time boundary.

## 2 — A session in a series shouldn't show a countdown

Five rows titled "Finish module 4 of python course," each with a different
countdown — 4 hours left, 28 hours left, 2 days — read as five competing
deadlines rather than one commitment spread across a week.

A countdown answers "how long until this is due." That's right for a
one-off deadline and wrong for a session in a series: the sessions have an
order, not five separate urgencies.

**For tasks belonging to a generated series** (`source == "prep"` or
`"commitment"` — they share a generator now), replace the countdown
subtitle with the day it belongs to. "Monday's session." "Wednesday's
session." Beyond a week out, a date instead of a weekday name.

Constraints:

- **Only generated series tasks.** Ordinary tasks keep their countdown.
- **Today's session should read as today** — "Today's session" rather than
  a weekday name.
- **An overdue session is a different case.** Once item 1 lands, a session
  only goes overdue if the day genuinely passed without it being done.
  Decide what it shows then and say why — a missed Monday session labelled
  "Monday's session" in red is arguably clearer than a countdown, but
  `DESIGN.md`'s never-shame rule applies and a wall of red past sessions is
  the failure mode we've avoided elsewhere.

This is the same readability problem that made seven identical "Study for
Thermodynamics final" rows unusable. One fix should cover both, since prep
and commitment tasks now share a generator — say whether it did.

## 3 — Ask whether to start today or tomorrow

Related to item 1: a commitment captured mid-afternoon currently generates a
first session for the remainder of today, which is usually not what the user
meant. Rather than guessing, ask.

**But only when both answers are genuinely available.** This flow is capped
at two questions for a reason — an interrogation after a brain dump is the
exact failure this app exists to avoid. So do **not** ask when:

- **Today has no room.** Captured at 10pm with a 2-hour session, tomorrow is
  the only possible answer. A question with one answer is noise.
- **The arithmetic forces it.** 6 hours due in 3 days, captured today —
  dropping today means the remaining sessions have to grow. There's no
  choice to offer, so state what's happening instead of asking.

Ask only when today has room *and* skipping it doesn't force the remaining
sessions longer.

**If the user picks tomorrow, the deadline holds and the work compresses.**
6 hours over 2 days is two 3-hour sessions, not a later deadline. Say so
plainly when it happens — the user chose the start day, not a longer
schedule, and finding out later that sessions grew is the kind of silent
change that erodes trust.

This question replaces neither existing question; it's asked in the same
closing message. Report what the flow looks like when all three could
apply, and whether that's too much — if it is, say which you'd drop.
