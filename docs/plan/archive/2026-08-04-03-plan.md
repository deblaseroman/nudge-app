## Why this exists

The app's stated purpose is to be the thing that notices time passing when
the user can't. Someone intends to learn Spanish for years, and nothing
ever surfaces that a year went by. Time blindness means elapsed time is
genuinely invisible — so something has to show it.

`NudgeGoal` already exists, written once at onboarding and read by
nothing. This makes it real.

**Tone note, and it supersedes the softer reading of `DESIGN.md`:** these
messages are *meant* to land with some weight. Showing someone a month has
passed is uncomfortable and that discomfort is the point — it's the
information they'd want. The rule that still holds: state the fact, don't
deliver a verdict. "It's been a month since you worked on Spanish" is a
fact. "You keep saying you'll do this" is a judgment. And every message of
this kind must end in something small and actionable, not just the gap.

## 1 — A Goals tab

A fifth tab for personal goals. Onboarding lands here.

- List of the user's goals, with when each was last worked on
- Add, edit, remove
- Empty state that invites setting one

`NudgeGoal` exists — report its current shape and what it's missing. Keep

Goals should also be settable through conversation eventually, but not in

## 2 — Track when a goal was last worked on

Nothing links tasks to goals, so "a month since you worked on it" is
currently unanswerable.

**Linking happens at capture.** The AI already classifies stakes, effort,
time window and commitment shape — add goal matching to the same call. The
user's active goals go into the prompt as context; a captured task that
plainly serves one gets linked. "Practice Spanish," "Duolingo," "Spanish
class" → the Spanish goal. Conservative: a wrong link corrupts the elapsed
count, a missed one just means a nudge fires slightly early.

**Track last activity** on the goal — completing a linked task, or running
a session on one. Report what you chose and where it's stored.

**The zero case is the important one.** A goal set at onboarding and never
worked on has no last-activity date, and that's precisely the user this
feature exists for. Elapsed time runs from goal creation when there's no
activity, and the copy must not imply a lapse that never started — "you
set this a month ago" rather than "it's been a month since you worked on
it."

## 3 — Bait and hook

The split, and it's the architectural point:

**The notification is bait.** Short, deterministic, from a template. It
says almost nothing — enough to get the app opened. Something in the
register of "Hey, we need to talk" but less ominous; propose two or three
options and say which you'd pick. An anxious user shouldn't brace for bad
news.

**The message box is the hook.** On open, it shows the real message —
expanded by default, not collapsed. This one is AI-written, and it's where
the weight belongs: the elapsed time, what they said they wanted, and a
small concrete offer.

The AI **reads** arbiter state to write it — which nudge fired, what's on
the list, what's been ignored — and **changes nothing** about arbiter
behavior. Reading for context, not deciding.

Two triggers, different weights:

- **Long lapse** — a month or more since a goal was worked on. Rare, and
  the sting is the point. Monthly at most per goal; make sure it can't
  become wallpaper.
- **Gentle invite** — a goal with nothing on the list. A short question in
  the message box asking whether to add something toward it today. No
  notification, no weight, just an offer.

## 4 — Turning a goal into a task

The offer has to be one tap. Accepting either message creates a task
linked to the goal, sensibly sized and named, without a form.

Say what duration and stakes it gets, and whether it's placed or left
unscheduled.

- Additive schema, property-level defaults, three schema lists.

## Evidence it worked

- The Goals tab lists, adds, edits, removes.
- A captured task about Spanish links to the Spanish goal; an unrelated
  one doesn't.
- Completing a linked task updates last-activity.
- A goal with no activity produces the zero-case copy, not a false lapse.
- The bait notification is short; the message box opens expanded with the
  full message.
- Accepting creates a linked task in one tap.
