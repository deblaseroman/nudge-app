## The idea

iOS fixes a local notification's text at **scheduling** time, hours or days
before it fires, while the app isn't running. So copy can't be generated at
delivery — but it can be generated *in advance*, for every case the arbiter
might need, and handed over for the arbiter to select from deterministically.

The AI writes. The arbiter decides. iOS delivers.

Three days of copy, because a user who stops opening the app is exactly the
one who still needs it working.

## 2 — Generate and cache nudge copy

Add a generation pass producing notification copy for the next **three
days**, across every kind the arbiter can schedule.

**What to generate.** Per-kind-per-task for the top candidates only — a
nudge that names the specific task is the whole point, but generating for
every task × every kind × three days is waste. Decide the cap and say what
you chose and why.

**Cache it** where the arbiter can read it at scheduling time, keyed so it
can find copy for a given kind and task. Survives cold launch.

**When to generate:**
- Once on the first open of a new calendar day — reuse the hook the
  placement rollover and calendar refresh already share
- On a real shift in what the day is about: a new commitment captured, an
  exam detected, a calendar import
- **Not** on every reevaluate. That fires dozens of times a day.

**Always fall back.** No key, no network, a failed call, an empty cache, or
a stale entry — the arbiter uses the existing deterministic template. This
is an enhancement over working copy, never a dependency. The app must
behave exactly as it does today when generation hasn't run.

**Validate at scheduling time.** Before using cached copy, confirm the named
task is still open and unfinished. A three-day-old nudge naming something
finished yesterday is worse than a generic one. Stale → template.

**DEBUG:** print how many generations ran today and what was cached. If it's
firing more than a few times a day the triggers are too loose and I want to
see that before it becomes a bill.

`DESIGN.md` governs every generated string — facts not verdicts, no
promised outcomes, no implied failure. Put the tone rules in the generation
prompt, not just in review.

## 3 — The come-back nudge

If the user hasn't engaged for three days, the last scheduled nudge in the
cached window becomes a **come-back**.

**Away means:** no app open, no notification tapped, no task checked off.
Acting on nudges without opening the app still counts as present — that's a
user the app is working for.

**Tone matters more here than anywhere.** Someone who stopped opening the
app may have had a bad week, and an app that notices their absence and
mentions it is one more thing telling them they failed. The copy should be
**factual and forward-looking, about what's coming rather than what was
missed.** "Chem quiz Friday" is a reason to come back. "You haven't opened
Nudge in three days" is a reproach with nothing to act on.

`DESIGN.md`'s never-shame rule is the spec. Show me the copy before it
ships.

**Event reminders continue regardless of absence** — they're budget-exempt
and factual, and a user who's disengaged still has class at nine. Confirm
nothing in this item can suppress them.

## 4 — Verify the three-day window actually holds

The arbiter cancels and rebuilds everything on each reevaluate. If the app
isn't opened for three days, no reevaluate runs — so whatever was scheduled
on day one must survive and fire on days two and three.

Confirm that's true. Report how many notifications are pending after a
single reevaluate, how far out the furthest is, and whether the iOS 64
pending-notification limit is anywhere near being hit with a realistic
schedule. That limit keeps the soonest 64 and silently drops the rest, and
a full class schedule plus three days of nudges is the shape that could
reach it.

If the window doesn't hold, say so — that's a finding, and it changes item
2's premise.
